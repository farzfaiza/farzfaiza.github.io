The need

Shown in my project deck

The brief said teachers should be able to monitor how often students ask to leave class, how long students are out, and where they go. The app should also create a line (queue) for students who asked to leave, and gather information about student activity so a teacher can tell whether someone is out of class for a troubling amount of time.

[ADD WHERE THIS BRIEF CAME FROM: an assigned prompt, teacher input, or both]

Persona

Shown in my project deck

Betty Lee is a persona: a composite character the team wrote to represent a teacher. She is not a research participant. She is a high school math teacher in Atlanta, Georgia, in her mid to late 30s.

Her problems

  • Typically looks for students after 15 minutes
  • Class is constantly interrupted by questions about leaving the room
  • Has to remember who asked first

Her needs

  • An organized queue
  • A way to keep track of which student has left and where they went
  • A way to see student history and who has been out the longest
  • A way to eliminate physical or paper hall pass sheets

The 15 minutes is a detail of the persona, not a measured result.

Shown in my project deck

The team looked at three existing apps: Hall Pass School Visitor Management, SmartPass and PassWhiz. The team's summary was that they have student profiles, track students' locations, and use a UI that is difficult to navigate.

Feedback on the prototype

Shown in my project deck

After the wireframes, UX researchers from the ICL user testing group reviewed the prototype and wrote their comments on sticky notes. The deck calls this step User Testing and acknowledges four reviewers. These were UX researchers giving mentorship-style feedback, not teachers, who are the app's intended users.

[CONFIRM HOW THE FEEDBACK WAS GIVEN: reviewing screens, or watching someone complete tasks. Add how many reviewers took part.]

What the nine notes said

  • Labels and orientation. Add a header and short description to each page. Name the login button "instructor login." Add a title to the teacher dashboard. The difference between two teacher dashboards was unclear, and so was the meaning of two square dots beside a class.
  • Navigation. The hamburger menu items and the navigation bar were unclear. One reviewer suggested opening navigation at the bottom instead.
  • The line. It was unclear where the line starts and ends. Reviewers asked whether each period needs its own line and whether a limit is needed on how many students can go to one place. One suggested putting a border around the picture of the student who is out, and ordering the rest by who is next.
  • Accounts and access. Reviewers asked how a substitute teacher would use the app, what the sign-in options are, and whether students would have accounts. One suggested single sign-on through the school, because student information is sensitive. Teachers also needed a way to choose how many classes appear on the dashboard.
  • Data. One reviewer asked how the data would be sorted and arranged.

Interpretation (my reading, not the data)

Most comments were about whether a person could tell where they were and what each control did, which points to labeling and navigation more than missing features. A smaller group were questions about who the users are and how they sign in.

Name, logo and visual design

Shown in my project deck

The team brainstormed names (Hall Passport, GoPass, Outpass, Passage) and settled on Class Q, with a logo of a location pin containing the letter Q.

The visual system uses four main colors and one typeface:

  • Eerie Black #191716, for text
  • Golden Rod #E6AF2E, an accent
  • Cultured #F6F6F4, the background
  • Deep Chestnut #B94848, an accent
  • Sofia Pro in Light, Regular and Bold

Limitations

  • The feedback came from UX researchers reviewing a prototype. As far as the deck shows, no teachers tested it, so it says little about how teachers would actually use the app.
  • The persona is a composite, and the deck does not document what it was based on.
  • There was no second round of feedback, so the effect of the design changes is unknown.
  • This is a prototype. It was not built or used in a school.
  • The palette was not tested for accessibility in the deck. Checking afterward, Golden Rod on Cultured has a contrast ratio of 1.84 to 1, which is too low for text, so gold works only as an accent. Eerie Black on Cultured is 16.5 to 1.
